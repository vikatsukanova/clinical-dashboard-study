Three jobs at once

Up to 600pxWork

The open assessment, or the form once it starts.

384pxResults

Exam, labs, ECG, echo, and cath, in that order.

The components exist to hold this chart. The catalog is the index, not the product.

Wide layout02
During a visit you need three things in front of you. Who the patient is, the work of this visit, and the results you use to decide. Those became the three columns. Patient is a fixed 320. The timeline stays centered and stops at 600, so a note does not stretch into a full-width paragraph. Results are 384. I want that picture in the room before I talk about atoms. The system is shaped by the chart. The catalog is how you find the parts.

One map for design and code

TemplateThe grid and the small-screen switchChart
PagesOne record, one viewDashboard, assessment
Atomic design03
Design and engineering share one map. Foundations are the tokens. An atom is one control and it does not invent layout. A molecule has one job, like a labeled field or a lab metric. An organism is a section of the chart. The template is the only place that knows the grid, and it also owns the small-screen switch between sections. The page fills that template for one view. Dashboard puts the timeline in the middle. Assessment puts the form there. Organisms do not navigate. The page passes start, back, and publish.

Atoms04
Each region has one primary button. It is the next step, Start assessment or Publish. Secondary saves a draft. Ghost is how you leave, Back. Then the distinction I hold people to: a chip names a fact, a status names where the work is. CAD is a diagnosis. In progress is a draft. They should not wear each other's clothes. Status writes its own label, In progress or Published, and the icon only repeats the words. The first chip in a group is the brand blue. The rest stay neutral, so the eye lands once.

Text pairs meet WCAG AA05
Color follows the clinical result. LDL-C coming down is good. eGFR coming down is attention, even though the arrow points the same way. A steady potassium stays neutral. I say that out loud because it is the rule people get wrong. The pairs also have to pass contrast. White on the brand blue passes. White on the published green passes. Amber on the yellow tile passes. Where a lighter green, yellow, or gray from the visual reference failed, I kept the meaning and moved one step darker. I did not turn the contrast rule off to keep a prettier hex.

The early type was flat, and the page had too much empty space. I pulled the hierarchy into two things that should win: the patient's name, and the numbers. Section titles are 14, medium weight, sentence case. They label the block. They do not compete with the name. Lab values are bold and tabular, and the unit stays on the number. A clinician should not have to remember whether that 2.1 is a cholesterol. Captions and dates sit in the secondary slate, the one that still passes contrast.

08 · Responsive

One section when the width drops

1041 and up

Three columns. 24 pixel gap. The bottom bar is hidden. The chart measures itself, so Storybook and a product frame behave the same way.

Timeline

The open assessment is the default section.

PatientTimelineClinical

At 1040 and under, a bar switches sections. At 720, the form actions stack.

Container queries on the chart08
The wide chart is the three columns. When the chart itself gets to 1040 or below, I show one section. The timeline is the default, because that is the work. A fixed bar at the bottom opens the patient record or the results. I did this instead of stacking all three. A stacked chart on a small screen is a long scroll through information you did not ask for. Below 720, the form buttons go full width so Publish is easy to hit. The queries hang off the chart, not the browser window. That is why the layout still responds inside a Storybook story.

09 · Accessibility

A failing story stays failed

Name

Icon buttons require a label. Status writes its own words. Fields and choices have visible labels.

Focus

A 2 pixel brand outline, 2 pixels out, on buttons, tabs, fields, choices, and the section bar.

Landmarks

Patient and results are labeled regions. The section bar is a navigation landmark.

Check

Axe runs on every story. A violation fails the story. The region rule is the only one off.

Addon, set to error09
Accessibility is part of the system, not a pass I do at the end. Every story runs axe, and the addon is set to error, so a violation fails the story. I fixed the real failures: contrast, names, landmarks. I did not disable rules to get a green check. The one exception is the region rule, because Storybook's canvas is not a page landmark. Icon buttons cannot be built without a label. Status announces In progress or Published. The clock and the check are decorative. Focus is always the same blue outline. And color is never the only signal. The lab still has its signed delta. The status still has its word.

10 · Two workflows

Same template, two pages

Dashboard Timeline in the work slot

Diagnoses, the open draft, published studies. Start assessment is the primary action.

Assessment Form in the work slot

Title, class, symptoms, tests, medication, note. Publish returns to the dashboard.

The patient column and the results stay put. Only the middle changes.

Pages10
This is the proof that the template is doing its job. Dashboard and assessment are the same three slots. The patient record stays. The results stay. The middle changes. On the dashboard the middle is the diagnosis timeline, and Start assessment is the primary. On the form, you set a title, a class, symptoms, tests, a medication change, and a note. Publish writes a title back onto the timeline and returns you to the dashboard. Back leaves without publishing. If we open Storybook after this, those are the two pages I would click through.
